Font audit — rl-0l1

Two families are loaded: Cormorant Garamond (the display serif — always italic, always light: the welcome title, the field-guide title, the park name in the strip) and Space Mono (everything else). Tate does not love the cursive-looking serif. Section A sets each candidate as a specimen, full-bleed, the way these words would actually sit on the walk's biggest surfaces. Section B keeps the in-app mockups for the mono. No site code changes until a pick is made.

Section A — the display serif, as specimens

These four places are the only ones the serif appears: two 48 px titles, a 22 px park name, a debug path. So the question is not legibility at small size, it is what the piece sounds like when it says its own name.

Cormorant Garamondcurrent

Resonant Landscapes

Custer State Park

Why it reads "cursive": drawn for display with hairline strokes, its italic is calligraphic and its light weight thins the hairlines further. Elegant on paper; at 22 px on a phone in sunlight, easy to read as handwriting.

EB Garamondclosest relative, sturdier

Resonant Landscapes

Custer State Park

Keeps the Garamond character but with real book proportions: more weight in the strokes, a truer italic — calligraphy with a spine, not a swoop. The smallest step away from what is there now: Tate would feel "less cursive", not "different font".

Loracontemporary, outdoorsy

Resonant Landscapes

Custer State Park

A workhorse serif with a brushed, confident italic. Less romantic, more grounded — pairs naturally with a mono because both carry visible tool marks. Holds up best at the 22 px strip size in motion and glare.

Newsreadereditorial

Resonant Landscapes

Custer State Park

Designed for news text at many optical sizes, so it is crisp at every size on screen. Its italic is restrained rather than calligraphic. Reads more "published" than "handwritten" — a quieter break from the current look than Lora.

Frauncesthe characterful one

Resonant Landscapes

Custer State Park

A soft old-style with deliberate wonk — a serif with personality rather than refinement. The most "artist's signage" of the set, which suits a piece about noticing a landscape. The risk is that it competes with the content instead of framing it.

Section B — Space Mono, for completeness

The complaint named the cursive one, and Space Mono is the other half of every screen: all tips, labels, buttons and captions. Two alternatives, same mockups: a field-guide tip at 12 px, an uppercase label at 11 px with 0.16em tracking, and the install button.

Constraints carried over

Every candidate is on Google Fonts (so the non-blocking load, the preconnects and the swap strategy in index.html carry over unchanged); every candidate ships woff2, so the service worker's globPatterns precache picks it up with no change; all support iOS 15/Safari 15; none touch zoom behaviour (WCAG 1.4.4). The one thing a swap does change is the fallback stack in tailwind.config and — for Fraunces and Newsreader — the font request URL gains variable axes, which is a slightly heavier request than Cormorant's static weights. Nothing here is a reason not to swap; it is what the swap PR will carry.
